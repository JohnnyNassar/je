02

The vendor's five steps

1

They open the page

joreption.com/bazar — no account, no login. The page opens in English or Arabic depending on their browser, and the whole layout flips to right-to-left for Arabic.

Behind it The shop itself is still hidden behind the Coming Soon splash. The bazaar page is deliberately exempt from that, so the event can be advertised while the store stays private.
2

They pick a weekend

A dropdown lists all 15 weekends, each showing how many tables are still free — Thu 23 – Fri 24 July — 88 free. Booking one weekend gets them both nights.

Behind it Choosing a weekend reloads the page rather than filtering in the browser, so the availability shown is always read fresh from the database — never a stale copy left over from when the page was first opened. Nights still exist as their own records underneath, so selling single days later is a settings change rather than a rebuild.
3

They tap a table on the plan

The plan above, with whichever tables are already taken greyed out. Picking one selects it and opens the form; they can change their mind before submitting.

4

They leave their details and paperwork

Name, phone and what they sell are required; shop name and product detail are optional. Anyone may attach a work or trade licence — and if they pick food, drinks, sweets or personal care, a health certificate becomes required and they cannot book without it. The total shows 30 JD + 10 JD deposit = 40 JD, payable in cash on the night.

Behind it The fee is read from the table's own record and the deposit from your settings — never from the submitted form, so neither can be altered by editing the page. The certificate rule comes from Article 31 of the leasing contract. They can attach up to four files of each kind — a licence and its renewal, say — adding more without losing what they already picked. Uploads are stored privately, outside the public web folder, and only you can open them. The vendor is also matched to an existing customer record by phone number, so someone who already shops with you isn't duplicated.
5

They get a confirmation

A page showing their table number, section, night, hours, and the amount due. The booking is requested, not yet guaranteed — you still have to confirm it.

Behind it That page is private: only the browser that made the booking can open it, or a logged-in staff member. Someone guessing at web addresses can't read another vendor's name and phone number.
03

What you do next

Every new request lands in the admin under Bazaar → Bookings, with a count badge in the sidebar so pending requests act as your to-do list.

PENDING → you confirm CONFIRMED / CANCELLED
StateMeaningDoes it hold the table?
PENDING A vendor asked for the table. The default for every new request. Yes — nobody else can take it while you decide.
CONFIRMED You've agreed it. The vendor keeps the table; you collect the 30 JD on the night. Yes

Each row also has a WhatsApp button that opens a chat with that vendor. It fixes up however they typed their number — 079…, +962…, 00962… all become a working link.

04

Why two vendors can't get the same table

This is the part worth understanding, because it's the one thing that would genuinely embarrass you on the night — two people turning up for table 42.

Say two vendors both want table 42 for the same weekend, and both press submit in the same second. Watch what happens:

Vendor A
book table 42 ✓ got it
The database
UNIQUE (weekend, table)
Vendor B
book table 42 ✗ rejected

Both requests are on their way.

The obvious way to build this is to check availability just before saving. That fails exactly here: both checks run before either save finishes, both see a free table, and both bookings go through. So the rule isn't enforced by the booking code at all — it's enforced by the database, which physically cannot store two live bookings for the same table in the same weekend.

The marker that makes it work is called active_slot. While a booking holds the table it's set to 1; when the booking is cancelled it's emptied.

Holding the table
active_slot = 1
Counts toward the rule. A second booking for the same table and night is rejected outright.
Cancelled
active_slot = empty
The database treats empty values as all different from one another, so any number of cancelled bookings can sit alongside one live booking.

So the guarantee holds however the booking was made — the public page, the admin panel, or two people clicking at once — while cancelled requests stay on file instead of being erased. The vendor who loses by a second sees "table 42 was just booked by someone else, please pick another" rather than an error.

05

What gets stored

weekends

One row per bookable weekend — 15 of them. This is what a vendor actually buys, and it can be closed to new bookings without deleting anything already booked for it.

nights

One row per trading night — 30, two per weekend. Carries the opening hours. Kept separate so single days could be sold later without rebuilding anything.

tables

One row per table — 100. Number, section, price per weekend, its position on the plan, and whether vendors may rent it. The twelve restaurant units are drawn but not rentable.

categories

What vendors may choose from, and which of those need a health certificate. Editable — add a category or change its rule without touching code.

bookings

One row per request. Which weekend, which table, the vendor's details and category, the fee and deposit agreed, whether the deposit has been returned, the state, and your private notes.

documents

Certificates attached to a booking — several per booking if the vendor has them. The files sit in storage/app/bazaar-documents, outside the public web folder, under random names, and are only ever released to a signed-in staff member.

06

Situations that come up

A vendor wants the same table every week

They book each weekend separately — there's no repeat option yet. Worth adding if regulars appear.

A food vendor turns up without a health certificate

They can't have booked online without uploading one — the form refuses it. If you took the booking by hand, the bookings list flags it and the Confirm dialog warns you before you approve.

Returning the deposit

You collect 40 JD on the night — 30 for the table, 10 held. After the weekend, if nothing is damaged, hit Deposit returned on the booking and it's recorded with the date. Each weekend shows how much you're still holding.

A vendor loses their confirmation page

There's no way for them to find it again on their own. They contact you, and you look them up in the admin by name or phone. A phone-based lookup, like order tracking has, would close this.

You need to cancel a weekend entirely

Switch it off in Bazaar → Weekends. It stops accepting new bookings while existing ones stay on record, so you still have everyone's number to call.

You want to charge a different price

Prices sit on each table, so you can charge more for a prime spot. Change one table, or select several and set them together. The deposit is a single setting for all bookings.

Nobody is told automatically

Confirming a booking doesn't message the vendor — you click the WhatsApp button yourself. Automatic confirmations would reuse the messaging settings already in the admin.
